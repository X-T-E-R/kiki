# Notification navigation

Notification clicks use the existing scope navigator and timeline locator. `HostNotification.scope` captures the producing window's credential-free `homeId`, `scopeId`, `serverHomeId`, and `connectionRef`; `route` carries the session, optional agent, and optional `turn` or `interaction` query. Session-list observations without an exact agent or frame do not invent one. Merged notifications open `/activity` in the captured scope.

`NavScopeBoundary` is the only click consumer. It verifies explicit destinations even in the active scope, preserves the dirty-draft confirmation and source visit, and shows the existing scope recovery surface for missing or changed targets. Missing explicit notifications are not treated as deleted back-stack entries. Active connections are validated without rebinding the session or reopening a remote connection. Preparation waits for a cancelled predecessor's rollback before starting the next scope change.

On Windows, `apps/kiki-gui/src-tauri/src/desktop_notifications.rs` uses the existing Windows 0.61 WinRT dependency directly. Before sending, the host registers its configured application identifier under the current user's `SOFTWARE\Classes\AppUserModelId`, including for portable and development executables. Do not borrow PowerShell's identity. A toast and its activation callback remain owned after the banner times out; neither timeout nor App focus acknowledges a conversation. The former `notify-rust` Windows `wait_for_response` consumer stopped at the first dismissal event, so it could not deliver a later Notification Center click. Other platforms retain `notify-rust` 4.18's typed response path: `Default` and the `open` action navigate, while dismissals, expiry and replies do not.

`HostNotification.sessionIds` and `scope` bind native entries to conversations. `useViewedSession` acknowledges the viewed session, including its child-agent routes, only while visible and focused. Windows removes matching entries from the banner and Notification Center; merged entries remain until all their sessions are acknowledged. `useAwayNotifications` also confirms a notification that finishes sending after the user has already opened its target. Scope changes invalidate old focus probes, and notification switches are checked again after a pending probe returns.

Each click has a monotonic `navigationId`; navigation acknowledgment clears only that id, independently of conversation acknowledgment. The Tauri adapter registers its live listener before draining pending clicks and discards duplicate or older deliveries. Cold page boot serializes the captured scope into Router state and verifies before mounting the target. Activation callbacks require the desktop process to remain running; reopening a page is not a test of activation after the entire desktop process exits.

The claim-matched fixtures are `src/host/{tauri,browser}.test.ts`, `src/lib/{awayNotify,useAwayNotifications,navScope,navReading}.test.ts(x)` in `apps/kiki-gui`, plus the notification tests in `src-tauri/src/lib.rs` and `src-tauri/src/desktop_notifications.rs`. `node apps/kiki-gui/scripts/visual-proof-completion-notification.mjs` walks the real GUI with a mocked native bridge: click routing, focus-gated acknowledgment, other-session retention and reconnect/reopen deduplication. It does not exercise an OS click.

`src-tauri/tests/native_notifications.rs` has opt-in WinRT retention/acknowledgment and delayed-click fixtures. Supply a unique task-owned `KIKI_NOTIFICATION_FIXTURE_AUMID` and `KIKI_NOTIFICATION_FIXTURE_OUTPUT`; first verify the identity's registry key does not exist, and restore only that exact task-created key after the run. The retention fixture checks immediately and after 12 seconds, then verifies that a different scope cannot remove an entry and acknowledging one conversation preserves the other. Run the delayed-click fixture with an interactive desktop driver, click the matching notification after its banner closes, and confirm its original route and scope. Native acceptance must separately click the body and action button with the target page live and booting, then check the exact space/session/agent/frame and return visit. Use an isolated candidate and test home; do not inject notifications into a user's running service.
